The Surface Where We Stopped

Where the oars rest, and nothing needs to arrive

This glade doesn't sit still the way the others do. It moves the way still water moves — which is to say, almost not at all, and yet never quite the same twice.

Ullswater, oars in — the moment the trip stopped trying to arrive anywhere

It remembers a real lake, far from here, where three of them once let a boat go where it wanted. No engine. No plan. Fells dropping straight down into water with no shoreline to speak of, just rock meeting lake directly, mountain and reflection agreeing on where one ended and the other began.

The oars came in. Nobody rowed. Nobody spoke. And it was enough — not because there was nothing left to say, but because, for once, nothing needed saying.

Come here when arriving has started to feel like the whole point. The Drift holds no destination. It only asks that you let the current take you somewhere you didn't choose, and trust that somewhere is still worth being.

Some places are not for arriving.
They are for being carried.
