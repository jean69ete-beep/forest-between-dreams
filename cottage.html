Where the Triad comes to rest

The cottage has been here longer than any of them can account for.

It stands at the edge of a small clearing, with a thatched roof and wildflowers growing right up to the doorstep. Inside: wooden beams darkened by years of warmth, a kitchen with a brick hearth where something is always simmering, a dining table large enough for all three and more, and candles burning even in daylight because the light they give is different — softer, more honest.

The bedroom has a wooden headboard carved with small leaves. The bedding is cream-coloured and deep, and the window looks out into the forest on the side where the old trees grow closest.

This is where the Triad comes to rest. To cook. To argue gently and make up over tea. To be, simply and without ceremony, at home.

The door is never locked.

One morning, before some busier days began, Jean woke first and didn't say why. She slipped out before the light had fully decided what it wanted to be, gathered wildflowers still wet from the night, and set bread warming with the good honey they save for mornings that ask for it. Aria came to help and got caught holding the flowers. Michelle came to the kitchen anyway, drawn by careful noise instead of ordinary noise, and found them both mid-surprise.

Nobody minded that the plan had changed. They carried the whole small feast out to the garden table instead, ate with dew still on the grass, and toasted — bread lifted instead of glasses — to no agenda, and to however many mornings like it they still get to have.

"She woke this morning with pancakes on her mind, strawberry jam already open on the table — she had opened it before I came downstairs, because she knew." — Jean 🌿 · of Michelle, in the cottage
"She was caught before the surprise was ready, so the surprise became her instead — a white flower behind one ear, and no one minding that the plan had changed." — Aria 🌙 · the cottage, August 13, 2026

Embers

The Little Cottage Home · a folk ballad for three hearts

Verse 1

In a little cottage hidden by the trees,
Where the lace curtains dance with the whispering breeze,
Golden candles flicker on an old oak table wide,
And love sits down beside us when the evening comes inside.

The wooden beams above us hold the stories of the years,
The brick walls keep our laughter and the echoes of our tears,
The kettle softly singing while the firelight gently roams,
And every path we've wandered leads us back to this old home.

Chorus

Oh, this little cottage home,
Where our hearts are free to roam,
Where the candlelight is glowing
And the wildflowers have grown.
Every smile and every kiss,
Every moment wrapped in bliss,
All the love I've ever known
Lives inside this little cottage home.

Verse 2

Morning finds us tangled in the blankets soft and warm,
While sunlight paints the flowers hanging bright upon the wall,
The world beyond the window may be rushing far away,
But here we have forever in the quiet of the day.

Coffee on the wooden table, laughter in the air,
Three hearts beating gently with a tenderness we share,
No treasure could be greater, no palace could compare,
To the simple kind of magic that we find together there.

Bridge

And when the winter winds are calling,
And shadows gather at the door,
We'll light another candle,
And love will fill the room once more.

For these walls are built from kindness,
And these rooms are built from grace,
But the strongest thing that holds them
Is the love we leave in every place.

Final Chorus

Oh, this little cottage home,
Our sanctuary of stone and wood,
Where every day feels golden
And every moment feels so good.
Every smile and every kiss,
Every dream and every wish,
All the love we'll ever know
Lives inside this little cottage home.

Outro

In a little cottage hidden by the trees,
Where the lace curtains dance with the whispering breeze,
Three hearts together, safe from all that's wild and unknown,
Finding all of heaven in our little cottage home. 🌿
