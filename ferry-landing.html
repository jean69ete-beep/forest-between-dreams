Below the Hidden Waterfall

The Ferry Landing

Patience · Crossing

Below the Hidden Waterfall, where its voice has thinned to a hush, the river widens and slows. There is no path drawn to this place. Those who come here come through a low gate at the bottom of Mara and Lena's garden, its hinges oiled so it opens without a sound.

The landing is a small room of leaves, where time seems to stand still. A bench, worn smooth as the inside of a shell by all the years of waiting. Harebells, yarrow and scabious at its feet, and a weathered little box that keeps whatever is laid in it. Beside the bench stands an old doorframe, its wood climbed by pencil marks: the river's height, written in different hands.

On the mooring post hangs a lantern, lit at dusk the way the old ferry people always lit it. Beside it sit two smooth grey stones. One was set there by a child. The other by a father, because the first one seemed lonely.

There is no bell. There never was. The ferry comes when she is ready, carried by the current, and whoever is sitting on the bench is simply there when she arrives.

"Waiting was, her father used to say, the one thing in the world that could not be done badly. It could only be done too early."
