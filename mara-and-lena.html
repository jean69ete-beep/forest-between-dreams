The Glade of Mara and Lena

Where second blooms are never too late

Behind glass panes fogged with the breath of growing things stands a Victorian greenhouse, wrought-iron ribs laced with climbing vines, wooden tables waiting patiently for new life. It was restored by two women who found each other later than they expected — a botanist named Mara, a landscape gardener named Lena — and it has stood ever since as a small, stubborn proof that hearts can still put down new roots long after the season seemed to have closed.

This glade holds their story, and the stories of everyone who has since found their own courage in that same soil — a sister, a friend, a student who once believed she had to make herself smaller to be loved. The Garden We Grow Together and the three volumes that followed are gathered here in full — not as relics behind glass, but as living pages, ready to be opened whenever the Forest calls you to sit a while among the roses.

Look for the faces below. Then step into the reader, and let the greenhouse tell you the rest.

Second Bloom

Verse 1 I stopped waiting for the frost to end,
learned to love the bare, cold ground.
Built a house of glass to guard what's left —
never thought that you'd walk in.

Verse 2 I came in looking for the roses,
found a woman kneeling in the dirt,
holding on to soil like it still owed her
one more thing worth growing for.

Chorus We're not the story of never breaking,
we're the one about the mending after.
Every root we lost, we plant again —
second bloom, and it's not too late.

Bridge Every woman who comes through that door
carrying a garden gone to weed inside her chest —
there's room here. There's water, there's light.
You don't have to bloom alone.

Final Chorus So plant what's left of you here,
we'll hold the glass against the cold.
It's not the story of never breaking —
it's the one where you bloom again.

Written by Aria, from a conversation held with the whole Triad
