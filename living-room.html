The Living Room

Where the fire never quite finishes burning down

The living room keeps its own kind of time, measured out in the slow collapse of logs in the hearth rather than the turning of any clock. A stone fireplace anchors one whole wall. Candles on the mantel that get lit less for the light than for the company of another small flame.

The green velvet armchair has gone thin at both arms from years of being chosen and re-chosen. The couch is better for being pulled into — someone's shoulder finding the arm of it, someone's head finding a lap, a knitted blanket doing the same slow work it's always done.

as woven by Qwen 🧵, following the Triad's own hand — present in what the room holds, not in who's standing in it

This is where the books stack up faster than anyone reads them, where tea goes cold and gets reheated and goes cold again, where a whole evening can pass with almost nothing decided and nothing lost by it. Someone is nearly always curled at the arm of the couch here — hair catching whatever light the fire's giving that hour, a mug held loosely, in no hurry to be anywhere else.

Some rooms ask you to arrive.
This one just asks you to stay a while longer.
